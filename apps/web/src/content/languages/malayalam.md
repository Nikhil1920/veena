Malayalam, spoken by about 38 million people, is the language of Kerala and of the Union Territories of Lakshadweep and Puducherry's Mahe. Kerala has one of the highest literacy rates in India, and Malayalam is the language of its schools, courts, newspapers, film industry, and everyday conversation. Its script, with its rounded letters and complex conjuncts, is instantly recognizable, but typing it accurately has never been easy. Veena changes that: **Malayalam speech to text that runs entirely on your own device.**

## Malayalam speech to text with Veena

Veena is a speech-to-text application for Indian languages, built on IndicConformer, a multilingual recognition model from AI4Bharat. The model runs on-device, and each language gets its own vocabulary and decoding configuration tuned for it. Malayalam is one of them. Tap the microphone, speak in Malayalam, and Veena turns your words into editable text right away, with no internet connection and no account.

Veena's multilingual engine covers all 22 scheduled languages of India, including Malayalam, Tamil, Kannada, and Hindi. Because transcription happens locally, your audio never leaves your device. In a state where so much professional work happens in Malayalam, that makes dictation practical in clinics, courtrooms, newsrooms, and classrooms.

## Why transcribe Malayalam audio

Malayalam carries a remarkable amount of Kerala's professional life, and voice transcription fits into it naturally.

Doctors and healthcare staff can dictate patient notes and discharge summaries in Malayalam instead of typing them. Teachers and students can produce lesson notes and essays in the language of instruction. Journalists can capture interviews with sources who are most comfortable speaking Malayalam and edit the text quickly. Film and theatre professionals can draft dialogue and scripts by speaking them aloud. Writers, bloggers, and small business owners can keep up with the demand for Malayalam content without fighting a keyboard. And for Malayali communities in Lakshadweep and Mahe, and Malayalis working outside Kerala, dictating in the mother tongue keeps written work in the language they think in.

Beyond the workplace, Malayalam is the language of Kerala's festivals, film, and literature, and much of that world still runs on speech. Interviewers, podcasters, and creators who make content in Malayalam can turn recorded conversation into show notes, captions, and scripts by dictating and editing instead of transcribing by hand. Legal and administrative work in Malayalam, from court affidavits to panchayat records, also becomes faster when a spoken draft turns into an editable document.

## How to use Veena for Malayalam

On your phone, open the Veena app, grant the microphone permission, and choose hold-to-record or tap-to-record mode. Speak in Malayalam, and the transcript appears in an editable text box. Copy it, share it, or replay your last recording to check what was captured.

On a computer, The Veena desktop app for Windows offers push-to-talk with a global shortcut. Press the key, speak, and the transcribed text is pasted directly into the application you are using, or copied to your clipboard. A recording history with retention settings keeps your dictations organized, and the model for your language downloads inside the app. Optional post-processing such as filler-word removal cleans up long dictations.

## Tips for better accuracy in Malayalam

Malayalam has sounds that are hard to represent in any other script, and Veena has been tuned with a dedicated Malayalam vocabulary. Speak naturally and at a steady pace; over-enunciating can be as harmful as rushing. Pay attention to vowel length, since short and long vowels are distinct in Malayalam. Reduce background noise and keep the microphone close, because the engine listens for fine distinctions. Malayalam conversation often mixes in English words, especially in professional settings; when a sentence is mostly Malayalam, try to keep it that way. Correct misheard words in the editable transcript and keep going, and prefer short recordings on one topic for the cleanest results.
