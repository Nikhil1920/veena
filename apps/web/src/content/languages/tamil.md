Tamil is the oldest living language of India, with a literary tradition that reaches back more than two thousand years to the Sangam era. Today it is spoken by about 75 million people across Tamil Nadu, Puducherry, and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, with large communities in Karnataka and Kerala as well. For a language with that kind of heritage and reach, voice typing should be a given — yet most Tamil speech-to-text tools depend on cloud services that need an internet connection and upload your audio to remote servers. Veena changes that. **Tamil speech to text runs entirely on your own device** — free, offline, and without an account.

## Tamil speech to text with Veena

Veena's multilingual engine covers all 22 scheduled languages of India, and Tamil is one of them. The recognition engine is built on IndicConformer, a multilingual model from AI4Bharat, with a dedicated vocabulary and decoding configuration for Tamil. Tap the microphone, speak in Tamil, and Veena converts your words into editable text in the Tamil script — instantly and offline.

Because the model runs on your device, there is no network round trip and no upload: a sentence like வணக்கம் (vanakkam, hello) becomes text the moment you finish speaking it. You can then edit, copy, or share the transcript, or paste it into whatever document or message you are working on.

## Why transcribe Tamil audio

Tamil is a language of schools, courts, cinema, journalism, and daily life, and there are plenty of reasons to turn speech into text:

- **Notes and study material.** Students and teachers can dictate lecture notes, study summaries, and assignments in Tamil instead of typing them.
- **Writing for work.** Emails, meeting notes, and reports in Tamil are faster to produce by speaking, especially when you are drafting in your head as you go.
- **Journalism and publishing.** Interviewers, reporters, and editors can transcribe spoken Tamil quickly, preserving quotes and details without the slog of manual typing.
- **Preserving the spoken language.** Recordings of elders' stories, family history, and community conversations become searchable written records when transcribed.
- **Everyday messages.** Long WhatsApp messages and notes in Tamil are easier to speak than to type on a phone keyboard.

No internet connection is needed for transcription, and no account is required. Your audio never leaves your device.

## Tips for better accuracy in Tamil

The Tamil vocabulary in Veena is tuned for the language as it is spoken today, and a few habits make the transcript cleaner:

- **Give vowels their full length.** Tamil distinguishes short and long vowels — க (ka) versus கா (kā), for example — and that distinction carries meaning. Speak at a natural pace and let each vowel hold its true length.
- **Keep the tricky consonants distinct.** Tamil has sounds that learners often confuse, like the two r-like consonants ர and ற and the retroflex ழ. When you pronounce them the way you would in careful speech, they come through correctly in the text.
- **Speak in complete sentences.** The model performs best on natural, connected speech; very short isolated words can be harder to place without context.
- **Keep background noise low.** A quiet room matters more than a fancy microphone; the model works best when your voice is the clearest thing it hears.
- **Edit before you copy.** The transcript box is fully editable, so fix any slip before you paste the text into an email, document, or message.
