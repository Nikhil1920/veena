Spoken by about 85 million people across Maharashtra, Goa, and the union territories of Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, Marathi (मराठी) is among the most widely spoken languages of India and the bearer of one of the country's great literary traditions. Written in the Devanagari script, it is the language of the Dnyaneshwari, of the abhang poetry of the saint-composers, of Marathi theatre, and of one of the busiest film industries in the world. For all that scale, Marathi voice typing has mostly meant cloud dictation — services that need an internet connection and that upload your audio to remote servers. Veena is built differently: **Marathi speech to text that runs entirely on your own device, with no account and no cloud.**

## Marathi speech to text with Veena

Veena's recognition engine is built on IndicConformer, a multilingual model from AI4Bharat, exported to ONNX and quantized so it runs entirely on-device. Veena's multilingual engine covers all 22 scheduled languages of India, and each language gets its own vocabulary and decoding tuned for it — Marathi included. Tap the microphone and speak in Marathi, and Veena records 16 kHz mono audio and transcribes it locally with the on-device model. The transcript appears in an editable text box, ready to copy, share, or refine. No internet connection is needed for transcription, and your audio never leaves your device.

A dedicated Marathi vocabulary matters because Marathi is not Hindi with a different accent. It has its own verb forms, gender inflections, case markers, and compound constructions, and everyday speech is full of words with no Hindi equivalent. A vocabulary and decoding setup tuned for Marathi is what lets the engine keep up with natural, colloquial speech, the way people actually talk from Mumbai to Nagpur to Kolhapur.

## Why transcribe Marathi audio

Marathi is the majority language of Maharashtra, the state that hosts Mumbai and some of the country's largest industries, so Marathi dictation turns up in all kinds of everyday work:

- Students and teachers in Marathi-medium schools and colleges can dictate notes, essays, and assignments instead of typing Devanagari on a small phone keyboard.
- Journalists and writers can speak their articles and interviews into Veena and get a first draft in Marathi, then edit it in the transcript box.
- Small business owners and shopkeepers can reply to customers in Marathi on chat and email without hunting for Devanagari keys.
- Researchers and historians recording interviews with native Marathi speakers get a written record without manual typing, even in places with patchy connectivity.
- Anyone who finds typing slow or uncomfortable — including people with limited hand mobility — can express themselves at the speed of speech.

Because Veena works fully offline, it is also a practical choice for field work, trains, flights, and rural areas where mobile data is expensive or unavailable. Your Marathi audio never has to leave your device to become text.

## How to use Veena for Marathi

On the mobile app, open Veena, tap the microphone, and speak in Marathi. The transcript appears in an editable text box — copy it, share it, or fix a word before sending. You can hold to record or tap to record, replay your last recording to check what you said, and edit transcripts after the fact. The app asks for only one permission: microphone access.

On the desktop app for Windows, use push-to-talk or a global shortcut to start dictating from any application. The audio is transcribed locally and the text is pasted directly into the app you are using, or copied to your clipboard. A local recording history helps you find past dictations, and the model for your language downloads inside the app. Optional post-processing such as filler-word removal and custom-word correction can be enabled; the transcription itself is fully on-device.

## Tips for better accuracy in Marathi

- Speak at a natural, steady pace and pause briefly between sentences.
- Keep the microphone close and background noise low — Veena handles 16 kHz mono audio, so a quiet room helps more than a fancier mic.
- Try to stay in one language per session. When English words are mixed into a Marathi sentence, the engine can lock onto a different word than you intended.
- Names of people, places, and brands often need a small edit; the transcript box makes that quick.
- Use the replay feature to hear your last recording if a sentence came out wrong.
- On desktop, enable custom-word correction for names and technical terms you dictate often.
