Bodo is spoken by about 1.5 million people, mainly in the Bodoland region of Assam, with speakers in Nagaland and Meghalaya as well. It is one of the 22 scheduled languages of India and is written in the Devanagari script — and it is exactly the kind of language that mainstream speech technology tends to skip. Big commercial voice products focus on languages with tens of millions of speakers, so a community of 1.5 million usually has to wait, or settle for tools that were never built for their language. Veena exists so that Bodo gets **Bodo speech to text** with the same care as the largest languages, running entirely on your own device. The [speech to text hub](/speech-to-text/) lists Bodo among the 22 scheduled languages Veena covers.

## Bodo speech to text with Veena

Veena's recognition engine is built on IndicConformer-600M-Multilingual from AI4Bharat, exported to ONNX and quantized so it can run entirely on-device. Veena's multilingual engine covers all 22 scheduled languages of India, and Bodo is transcribed with a dedicated vocabulary and decoding tuned for it. Tap the microphone, speak in Bodo, and the transcript appears in an editable text box in the Devanagari script — with no internet connection and no cloud account involved.

Smaller languages are where on-device processing matters most. A Bodo speaker in a village in Bodoland may have patchy connectivity; cloud transcription would fail exactly where it is needed. Veena records 16 kHz mono audio and transcribes it locally with the on-device model, so the quality of your connection does not affect the quality of your transcript. Your audio never leaves your device, and that is true whether you dictate in Kokrajhar or in a remote corner of Nagaland.

## Why transcribe Bodo audio

Every language community benefits when its speakers can produce text by voice, but the benefit is sharper for a smaller language:

- Bodo-medium schools in the Bodoland region: teachers can prepare lessons and worksheets by dictating, and students can take notes in Bodo without slow typing.
- Local administration and offices that work in Bodo can produce letters and records faster by voice.
- Journalists covering the Bodoland Territorial Region can file stories in Bodo, hands-free.
- Writers and poets working in Bodo can draft and revise manuscripts by dictating.
- Families can transcribe folk songs, stories, and interviews in Bodo, preserving them as text for younger generations.
- Content creators making Bodo videos and audio programs can generate scripts and captions by voice.

Underneath all of these is a simple point: a language with 1.5 million speakers deserves native speech to text. When speakers can dictate, they write more in their own language instead of switching to Assamese or English — and that keeps the language alive in everyday written use.

## Tips for better accuracy in Bodo

Getting clean Bodo transcripts is mostly about giving the engine consistent audio:

- Speak in complete sentences. Recognition works best when words have context around them.
- Keep a steady, natural pace and pause between sentences.
- Reduce background noise and keep the microphone close — a headset helps on desktop.
- Avoid mixing languages mid-sentence. Bodo speakers often switch into Assamese, Bengali, or English; each utterance is transcribed most accurately when it stays in one language.
- Speak in your natural accent rather than trying to sound formal.
- Use the replay feature for names and unusual words, and edit the transcript directly when a spelling needs a fix — the transcript is fully editable.
