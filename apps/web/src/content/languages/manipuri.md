Manipuri, also known as Meitei, is spoken by about 2 million people in Manipur and by Meitei communities in Assam, Tripura, and Nagaland. It is one of the 22 scheduled languages of India and the language of everyday life in the Imphal valley. Manipuri is written in two scripts: Meitei Mayek, which is the official script and is taught in schools, and Bengali script, which remains common in everyday writing and in older publications. Voice transcription is a natural fit for a language with this kind of written diversity: you speak, and the technology handles the rest. Veena changes that: **Manipuri speech to text that runs entirely on your own device.**

## Manipuri speech to text with Veena

Veena is a speech-to-text application for Indian languages, built on IndicConformer, a multilingual recognition model from AI4Bharat. The model runs on-device, and each language gets its own vocabulary and decoding configuration tuned for it. Manipuri is one of them. Tap the microphone, speak in Manipuri, and Veena turns your words into editable text right away, with no internet connection and no account.

Veena's multilingual engine covers all 22 scheduled languages of India, including Manipuri, Bengali, Assamese, and Hindi. Because transcription happens locally, your audio never leaves your device. That makes Veena practical for journalism, education, and community documentation, where recordings are often personal or sensitive.

## Why transcribe Manipuri audio

Manipuri is the language of schools, courts, markets, and homes across the Imphal valley, yet written material in the language still lags behind what people speak every day. Voice transcription closes that gap.

Students can dictate essays and assignments in the language of their classrooms. Teachers and writers can compose lesson material and articles in Manipuri without struggling with typing. Journalists covering Manipur can interview sources in their own language and turn the recordings into text for publication. Cultural groups documenting Meitei oral traditions, rituals, and folk narratives can build searchable text archives. Administrators and small business owners can keep notes, minutes, and correspondence by dictating them.

The script question is part of the picture. Meitei Mayek is the official script and is taught in schools, while Bengali script remains widespread in daily use and in older texts. Being able to dictate in Manipuri means the spoken language no longer depends on which writing system you happen to type in.

That matters most for students. Schools teach Meitei Mayek as the official script, while older publications and much everyday writing still use Bengali script. Dictating in Manipuri lets students produce text in the language itself, whichever script they end up working with.

## Writing Manipuri in Meitei Mayek

The Meitei Mayek script has its own alphabet of 27 letters, plus numeral and consonant marks, and typing it on a standard keyboard means hunting for characters through a mapper app. Dictation skips that step. You speak Manipuri, and the transcript appears as editable text; if you write in Meitei Mayek, you are producing the script by speaking rather than by tapping.

For anyone searching how to write Manipuri in its own script, dictation is the practical answer: it is faster than character-by-character typing, it works on a phone, and it keeps your spelling consistent because the model writes the words the way they sound. Everyday words like ꯃꯤꯇꯩꯂꯣꯟ (Meiteilon, the language's own name for itself) come out letter by letter without you hunting for a single character on a keyboard. Fix anything that slips, then copy the text wherever it needs to go.

## Tips for better accuracy in Manipuri

Speak at a natural, steady pace and finish your words. Manipuri has its own rhythm and tonal character, and Veena has been tuned with a dedicated Manipuri vocabulary; consistent speech gives it the best signal. Keep background noise low and the microphone close. Try to avoid switching between Manipuri and Bengali, Hindi, or English inside a single sentence, since code-mixing confuses any speech engine. If a word is misheard, correct it in the editable transcript and continue. Short recordings on a single topic tend to transcribe more reliably than long monologues that jump between subjects.
