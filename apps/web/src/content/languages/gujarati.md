Gujarati is spoken by about 60 million people in Gujarat and the union territories of Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, and it is written in the Gujarati script, which has its own distinctive letterforms and vowel signs. It is a language of trade, literature, cinema, and one of the largest diasporas in the world — yet for Gujarati speakers in India, dictation has usually meant cloud services that may not even support the language. Veena changes that: **Gujarati speech to text** that runs entirely on your own device, free and offline. ## Gujarati speech to text with Veena

Veena's recognition engine is built on IndicConformer-600M-Multilingual from AI4Bharat, exported to ONNX and quantized so it can run entirely on-device. Veena's multilingual engine covers all 22 scheduled languages of India, and Gujarati is transcribed with a dedicated vocabulary and decoding tuned for it. Tap the microphone, speak in Gujarati, and within moments the transcript appears in an editable text box in the Gujarati script — with no internet connection required.

The script detail matters because it is what makes the transcript usable. Gujarati has its own vowel marks and conjunct forms, and Veena writes what you say in the Gujarati script rather than a transliteration, so the result reads the way a Gujarati speaker expects. If a word comes out spelled differently than you intended, the transcript is fully editable — tap the word and correct it without re-dictating. And because everything happens on-device, your audio never leaves your device; Veena records 16 kHz mono audio and transcribes it locally with the on-device model.

## Why transcribe Gujarati audio

Gujarat is a hub of trade and small business, and much of that activity happens in Gujarati. Dictation fits naturally into that world:

- Business owners and traders dictating letters, bills, and order notes in Gujarati instead of typing them out.
- Students and teachers using Gujarati-medium education, taking notes and preparing material by voice.
- Journalists and writers drafting articles, columns, and books in Gujarati.
- Doctors and clinic staff recording patient summaries in Gujarati.
- Content creators making Gujarati videos and podcasts, dictating scripts and captions.
- Families and community groups transcribing interviews, songs, and oral histories in Gujarati.

Underneath the use cases is a practical fact: typing Gujarati on a phone keyboard is slow, because of the script's vowel signs and conjuncts. Speaking is faster, and voice transcription removes the main reason people fall back to English when they want to write quickly. For anyone who finds keyboards difficult, dictation also opens up writing in Gujarati who could not type it comfortably before.

## Tips for better accuracy in Gujarati

Gujarati accents vary across the state, and a few habits keep transcripts clean regardless of where you are from:

- Speak in your natural accent. The engine is tuned for everyday Gujarati, not for a broadcast style.
- Use complete sentences so the engine can use context to pick the right words.
- Keep a steady pace and pause between sentences.
- Reduce background noise and keep the microphone close — a headset helps on desktop.
- Avoid switching languages mid-sentence. Gujarati speakers often mix in English or Hindi words; accuracy is highest when each utterance stays in one language.
- Replay tricky passages and edit the transcript directly when a spelling needs correction.
