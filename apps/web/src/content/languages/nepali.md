Nepali (नेपाली) is spoken by about 16 million people in India — chiefly in Sikkim, the Darjeeling hills of West Bengal, Assam, and Uttarakhand — and it is also the national language of Nepal, where tens of millions more speak it every day. Written in the Devanagari script, Nepali carries a rich tradition of poetry, prose, and journalism across the Himalayas and the eastern plains. Despite that reach, Nepali voice typing has mostly meant cloud dictation — services that need an internet connection and that upload your audio to remote servers. Veena is built differently: **Nepali speech to text that runs entirely on your own device, with no account and no cloud.**

## Nepali speech to text with Veena

Veena's recognition engine is built on IndicConformer, a multilingual model from AI4Bharat, exported to ONNX and quantized so it runs entirely on-device. Veena's multilingual engine covers all 22 scheduled languages of India, and each language gets its own vocabulary and decoding tuned for it — Nepali included. Tap the microphone and speak in Nepali, and Veena records 16 kHz mono audio and transcribes it locally with the on-device model. The transcript appears in an editable text box, ready to copy, share, or refine. No internet connection is needed for transcription, and your audio never leaves your device.

A dedicated Nepali vocabulary matters because Nepali is not a dialect of Hindi. Its sound system, verb forms, honorifics, and everyday vocabulary are its own, and Devanagari text produced from a Hindi model would be full of wrong words. Veena's Nepali model is tuned for the language itself, so natural spoken Nepali — the way it is used in Gangtok, Darjeeling, Dehradun, or Kathmandu — becomes editable Devanagari text on your screen.

## Why transcribe Nepali audio

Nepali speakers in India live in communities where the language is central to schools, press, and family life, and dictation fits naturally into that daily rhythm:

- Students in Sikkim and Darjeeling can dictate essays and homework in Nepali without slow Devanagari typing on a phone.
- Journalists and community publishers can speak their reports and interviews into Veena and get a first draft in Nepali, ready to edit.
- Writers of Nepali poetry and prose can capture spoken drafts anywhere — on a bus, in a tea shop, or on a mountain trail — because no connection is needed.
- Translators and researchers working between Nepali, Hindi, and English can produce Nepali text quickly and refine it in the transcript box.
- Families keeping up Nepali correspondence across India and Nepal get fast, editable messages in their own language.

Because transcription happens fully on-device, it also works where connectivity is unreliable — hill towns, remote villages, and long journeys included. Your Nepali audio never has to leave your device to become text.

## How to use Veena for Nepali

On the mobile app, open Veena, tap the microphone, and speak in Nepali. The transcript appears in an editable text box — copy it, share it, or fix a word before sending. You can hold to record or tap to record, replay your last recording to check what you said, and edit transcripts after the fact. The app asks for only one permission: microphone access.

On the desktop app for Windows, use push-to-talk or a global shortcut to start dictating from any application. The audio is transcribed locally and the text is pasted directly into the app you are using, or copied to your clipboard. A local recording history helps you find past dictations, and the model for your language downloads inside the app. Optional post-processing such as filler-word removal and custom-word correction can be enabled; the transcription itself is fully on-device.

## Tips for better accuracy in Nepali

- Speak at a natural, steady pace and pause briefly between sentences.
- Keep the microphone close and background noise low — a quiet room helps more than a fancier mic.
- Stick to one language per session. Mixing Hindi or English into a Nepali sentence can make the engine pick a different word than you intended.
- Honorifics and kinship terms (for example, the respectful forms used when addressing elders) are worth dictating slowly and clearly; the transcript box makes any correction quick.
- Names of people, places, and brands often need a small edit.
- Use the replay feature to hear your last recording if a sentence came out wrong.
- On desktop, enable custom-word correction for names and terms you dictate often.
