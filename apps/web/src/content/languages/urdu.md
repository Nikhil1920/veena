Urdu is spoken by about 50 million people in India — across Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Telangana (especially Hyderabad), Delhi, and Jammu & Kashmir — making it one of the most widely spoken languages of the country. It is written in the Perso-Arabic script in the flowing Nastaliq style, the calligraphic script of Urdu poetry and prose. Urdu and Hindi share a spoken base: much of everyday conversation is mutually intelligible, yet the two languages are written in completely different scripts. Most speech-to-text tools either ignore Urdu or transliterate it into another script. Veena does neither. **Urdu speech to text runs entirely on your own device** — free, offline, and without an account.

## Urdu speech to text with Veena

Veena's multilingual engine covers all 22 scheduled languages of India, and Urdu is one of them. The recognition engine is built on IndicConformer, a multilingual model from AI4Bharat, with a dedicated vocabulary and decoding configuration for Urdu.

The key detail: Veena's Urdu speech recognition preserves the Urdu script rather than transliterating. Speak Urdu and the transcript comes back in اردو — the Perso-Arabic Nastaliq script — not as a Latin or Devanagari approximation. That means the text is immediately usable in Urdu documents, messages, and publications, without a conversion step. A courtesy like شکریہ (shukriya, thank you) appears exactly as you would write it.

## Why transcribe Urdu audio

Urdu has a deep literary tradition and an active daily presence across India, and there are plenty of reasons to turn speech into text:

- **Journalism and publishing.** Reporters and editors can transcribe Urdu interviews and recordings quickly, preserving quotes and details in the script they publish in.
- **Literature and poetry.** Poets and writers can dictate couplets, drafts, and prose directly into Urdu text, capturing the flow of composition without breaking it to type.
- **Education.** Students and teachers can produce notes and study material in Urdu, the medium of instruction in many schools and madrasas.
- **Administrative work.** Urdu remains a working language in courts and offices across northern India; dictation makes correspondence and records faster to produce.
- **Everyday messaging.** Long messages and notes in Urdu are easier to speak than to type on a phone keyboard, especially for readers who do not type the script regularly.

## Tips for better accuracy in Urdu

The Urdu vocabulary in Veena is tuned for the language as it is spoken in India, and a few habits make the transcript cleaner:

- **Speak at a natural pace.** Urdu is spoken quickly in everyday conversation; a steady, natural pace gives the model clean words to work with, while rushed speech blurs them together.
- **Choose your register deliberately.** Colloquial Urdu and the formal register of newspapers and literature differ in vocabulary. Speak the way you want the text to read, and the dedicated Urdu vocabulary follows your words.
- **Keep sentences in Urdu.** Because Urdu and Hindi share so much spoken vocabulary, switching languages mid-sentence makes the transcript more likely to blur; a sentence in one language gives the clearest result.
- **Let proper nouns breathe.** Names of people and places are the hardest part of any transcription; say them clearly and check them in the editable transcript before you copy.
- **Keep background noise low.** A quiet room matters more than a fancy microphone; the model works best when your voice is the clearest thing it hears.
- **Edit before you copy.** The transcript box is fully editable, so fix any slip before you paste the text into a document, message, or publication.
