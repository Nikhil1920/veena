Maithili, spoken by about 35 million people, is the language of the Mithila region that spans northern Bihar, parts of Jharkhand, and the Terai of Nepal. It is one of the 22 scheduled languages of India and has a literary heritage that includes the poetry of Vidyapati and a rich tradition of folk song. Today Maithili is written in Devanagari, while the historical Tirhuta script still appears in ceremonial and religious contexts. For a language with this much literary life, the everyday tools for turning speech into text have been slow to arrive. Veena changes that: **Maithili speech to text that runs entirely on your own device.**

## Maithili speech to text with Veena

Veena is a speech-to-text application for Indian languages, built on IndicConformer, a multilingual recognition model from AI4Bharat. The model runs on-device, and each language gets its own vocabulary and decoding configuration tuned for it. Maithili is one of them. Tap the microphone, speak in Maithili, and Veena turns your words into editable text right away, with no internet connection and no account.

Veena's multilingual engine covers all 22 scheduled languages of India, including Maithili, Hindi, Bengali, and Nepali. Because transcription happens locally, your audio never leaves your device. That makes Veena comfortable to use for personal notes, family letters, and community work alike.

## Why transcribe Maithili audio

Maithili is spoken by tens of millions of people, but much of its written life still happens on keyboards designed for other languages. Voice transcription gives the spoken language a direct path into text.

Students who study Maithili as a subject can dictate essays and practice passages. Teachers and writers can compose in Maithili without slowing down for typing. Journalists covering the Mithila region can capture interviews in the language of their sources and file stories faster. Families documenting songs, rituals, and oral histories, from wedding traditions to folk tales, can build text archives that will outlive recordings. Agricultural cooperatives and small businesses in the region can keep meeting notes and ledgers by dictating them in the language everyone speaks.

The Mithila region is also home to a strong tradition of folk song and ritual verse, from wedding songs to festival hymns, and these are still passed down orally. Dictation lets families and cultural groups capture that material as text for the first time without an intermediary. Community libraries, local publishers, and organizations working in Maithili can build searchable collections, and families spread across Bihar, Jharkhand, and Nepal can keep a written record of the language their elders speak.

## How to use Veena for Maithili

On your phone, open the Veena app, grant the microphone permission, and choose hold-to-record or tap-to-record mode. Speak in Maithili, and the transcript appears in an editable text box. Copy it, share it, or replay your last recording to check the result.

On a computer, The Veena desktop app for Windows offers push-to-talk with a global shortcut. Press the key, speak, and the transcribed text is pasted directly into the application you are using, or copied to your clipboard. A recording history with retention settings helps you keep track of dictations, and the model for your language downloads inside the app. Optional post-processing such as filler-word removal keeps long dictations readable.

## Tips for better accuracy in Maithili

Maithili shares the Devanagari script with Hindi, but its sound system is its own, and Veena has been tuned with a dedicated Maithili vocabulary. Speak at a natural pace and avoid mixing Hindi into the middle of a Maithili sentence, since code-switching can trip up any speech engine. Enunciate clearly and let long vowels carry their full length, because vowel length matters in Devanagari orthography. Record in a quiet room and keep the microphone close. If a word is misheard, correct it in the editable transcript and continue; short, topic-focused recordings usually give cleaner results than long monologues.
