Hindi is the most widely spoken language of India, with more than 500 million speakers across the Hindi belt — Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, and beyond. It is written in the Devanagari script, a phonetic script built from vowel signs and consonant characters that combine into complex syllables. Despite Hindi's scale, most Hindi voice tools are cloud-based: they need an internet connection and upload your audio to a remote server. Veena offers **Hindi speech to text** that runs entirely on your own device — free, private, and fully offline. The [speech to text hub](/speech-to-text/) explains how Veena covers all 22 scheduled languages of India.

## Hindi speech to text with Veena

Veena's recognition engine is built on IndicConformer-600M-Multilingual from AI4Bharat, exported to ONNX and quantized so it can run entirely on-device. Veena's multilingual engine covers all 22 scheduled languages of India, and Hindi is transcribed with a dedicated vocabulary and decoding tuned for it. Tap the microphone, speak in Hindi, and within moments the transcript appears in an editable text box in Devanagari — with no internet connection needed and nothing uploaded anywhere.

A word about Hinglish. Everyday Hindi speech is full of English words — meetings, technology, brand names — and Veena transcribes what it hears as faithfully as it can. But accuracy is highest when each utterance stays in one language. If you dictate a sentence that switches between Hindi and English mid-way, the transcript is more likely to need edits. For a clean Devanagari result, dictate one sentence in Hindi, then a separate sentence in English when you need both.

And a word about dialects. Hindi is not one uniform accent: the Hindi of western Uttar Pradesh sounds different from the Hindi of Bihar or Rajasthan. Veena's Hindi model is trained on speech data from across the Hindi belt, so the major regional accents are handled. Speak in your natural voice rather than putting on a formal accent, and the engine will do better than if you try to sound like a newsreader.

## Why transcribe Hindi audio

Typing Hindi in Devanagari on a phone keyboard is slow: matras and consonant clusters turn every word into several keystrokes. Voice removes that friction, and the use cases are as broad as the language itself:

- Students and teachers dictating notes, assignments, and lesson material in Hindi.
- Professionals in offices across the Hindi belt producing emails, reports, and meeting notes by voice.
- Journalists and writers drafting articles and scripts in Hindi, hands-free.
- Doctors and clinic staff recording patient summaries in Hindi.
- Government and administrative workers who operate in Hindi producing documents faster.
- Content creators making Hindi videos and podcasts, dictating scripts and captions.

Dictation also helps accessibility: people who cannot type comfortably can still write in their own language. And because Veena works offline, it keeps working in areas with weak connectivity, on trains, or anywhere you would rather not send your speech to a server.

## Tips for better accuracy in Hindi

Hindi transcription improves quickly with a few consistent habits:

- Speak in complete sentences. The engine uses context, so full sentences beat isolated words.
- Keep a steady, natural pace and pause between sentences.
- Stay in one language per utterance. For the cleanest Devanagari transcript, avoid mixing English or Hinglish words into the middle of a Hindi sentence.
- Speak in your natural regional accent instead of a formal or broadcast style.
- Reduce background noise and keep the microphone close — a headset helps on desktop.
- For names, addresses, and technical terms, use the replay feature to check, and edit the transcript directly when a word needs correcting.
