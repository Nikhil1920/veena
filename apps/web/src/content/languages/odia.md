Spoken by about 40 million people across Odisha, Jharkhand, West Bengal, and Chhattisgarh, Odia (ଓଡ଼ିଆ) is one of the major languages of eastern India, written in its own rounded script that sets it apart at a glance. It is the language of Odisha's schools, courts, newspapers, and cinema, and it carries a literary tradition that goes back a thousand years. Yet for all that depth, Odia voice typing has mostly meant cloud dictation — services that need an internet connection and that upload your audio to remote servers. Veena is built differently: **Odia dictation and speech to text that run entirely on your own device**, with no account and no cloud.

## Odia dictation with Veena

Veena's recognition engine is built on IndicConformer, a multilingual model from AI4Bharat, exported to ONNX and quantized so it runs entirely on-device. Veena's multilingual engine covers all 22 scheduled languages of India, and each language gets its own vocabulary and decoding tuned for it — Odia included. Tap the microphone and speak in Odia, and Veena records 16 kHz mono audio and transcribes it locally with the on-device model. The transcript appears in an editable text box, ready to copy, share, or refine. No internet connection is needed for transcription, and your audio never leaves your device.

A dedicated Odia vocabulary matters for two reasons. First, the Odia script is distinct — the text must be produced in Odia letters, not in a related script with a different shape. Second, Odia grammar and vocabulary are their own: its verb system, case endings, and everyday words differ from Bengali and Hindi, so a model tuned for a neighboring language would garble natural speech. Veena's Odia model is tuned for Odia itself, from the way people talk in Bhubaneswar and Cuttack to the varieties spoken in western Odisha and across the state's borders.

## Why transcribe Odia audio

Odia is the working language of a whole state, and Odia speakers are also found in large communities beyond Odisha. That makes dictation useful in many everyday situations:

- Students in Odia-medium schools can dictate notes and essays instead of typing the Odia script character by character on a phone.
- Government and office workers in Odisha can draft letters, reports, and meeting notes in Odia quickly, then edit them in the transcript box.
- Journalists, editors, and writers can speak articles, interviews, and stories into Veena and get a first draft in Odia.
- Publishers and translators working on Odia literature can capture spoken drafts anywhere, even without a connection.
- People sending Odia messages to family and colleagues get fast, editable text without wrestling with an unfamiliar keyboard layout.

Because Veena works fully offline, it is also a practical choice for field reporting, travel, and areas where mobile data is expensive or unreliable. Your Odia audio never has to leave your device to become text.

## How to use Veena for Odia

On the mobile app, open Veena, tap the microphone, and speak in Odia. The transcript appears in an editable text box — copy it, share it, or fix a word before sending. You can hold to record or tap to record, replay your last recording to check what you said, and edit transcripts after the fact. The app asks for only one permission: microphone access.

On the desktop app for Windows, use push-to-talk or a global shortcut to start dictating from any application. The audio is transcribed locally and the text is pasted directly into the app you are using, or copied to your clipboard. A local recording history helps you find past dictations, and the model for your language downloads inside the app. Optional post-processing such as filler-word removal and custom-word correction can be enabled; the transcription itself is fully on-device.

## Tips for better accuracy in Odia

- Speak at a natural, steady pace and pause briefly between sentences.
- Keep the microphone close and background noise low — a quiet room helps more than a fancier mic.
- Stay in one language per session. English and Hindi words mixed into an Odia sentence can send the engine down the wrong path.
- Odia has pairs of similar-sounding consonants and long vowels that change meaning; articulating clearly helps the engine keep them apart.
- Names of people, places, and organizations often need a small edit; the transcript box makes that quick.
- Use the replay feature to hear your last recording if a sentence came out wrong.
- On desktop, enable custom-word correction for names and terms you dictate often.
