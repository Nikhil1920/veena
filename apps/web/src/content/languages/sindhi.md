In India, Sindhi is spoken by about 3 million people, most of them in the Kutch region of Gujarat and in Rajasthan, with communities in Maharashtra and Delhi. It is a language with two scripts: Sindhi is written in the Perso-Arabic script and also in Devanagari, a reflection of its long history on both sides of the border. As a scheduled language of India, Sindhi deserves the same speech-to-text support as any major language — but most voice typing tools pass over it entirely. Veena does not. **Sindhi speech to text runs entirely on your own device** — free, offline, and without an account.

## Sindhi speech to text with Veena

Veena's multilingual engine covers all 22 scheduled languages of India, and Sindhi is one of them. The recognition engine is built on IndicConformer, a multilingual model from AI4Bharat, with a dedicated vocabulary and decoding configuration for Sindhi. When you speak Sindhi, Veena transcribes it as Sindhi, with the words, sounds, and rhythm of the language as it is spoken in India.

The transcript appears in an editable text box, ready to copy or share. Whether you are answering a message, keeping notes, or drafting a document, you can speak first and worry about the text afterward. Even a simple courtesy like مهرباني (mehrbani, thank you) flows straight into your text, exactly as you said it.

## Why transcribe Sindhi audio

Sindhi communities in India are comparatively small, which makes the language easy for mainstream technology to overlook — and all the more valuable to support:

- **Preserving the language.** Recording and transcribing Sindhi speech — family stories, community events, oral history — creates a written record for younger generations who read more than they listen.
- **Cultural documentation.** Kutch is home to a rich tradition of Sindhi song, poetry, and narrative; transcripts make that material accessible to readers and researchers.
- **Education.** Sindhi is taught in schools in Gujarat and Rajasthan, and teachers can turn spoken lessons into written material faster by dictating.
- **Everyday writing.** Letters, greetings, and notes to relatives who read Sindhi are easier to produce by speaking than by slowly typing in a script you are not used to typing.

For a language with millions of speakers but little commercial attention, a dependable transcription tool makes a practical difference.

## How to use Veena for Sindhi

Open Veena, tap the microphone, and start speaking Sindhi. The app records 16 kHz mono audio and transcribes it locally with the on-device model, then shows the transcript in an editable text box.

- On the **mobile app** (Android and iOS), use hold-to-record for short bursts or tap-to-record for longer dictation, then copy the transcript or share it.
- On the **desktop app** (Windows; macOS and Linux are in development), use the push-to-talk shortcut and the text is pasted directly into the app you are using, or copied to your clipboard.
- You can **replay the last recording** to check what was said, and every transcript can be edited before you copy it.

No internet connection is needed for transcription, and no account is required. Your audio never leaves your device.

## Tips for better accuracy in Sindhi

The Sindhi vocabulary in Veena is tuned for the language as it is spoken in India, and a few habits make the transcript cleaner:

- **Speak in your own Sindhi.** Regional accents vary across Kutch, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, and Delhi; speak the way you naturally do, at your normal pace, and the model follows your words rather than a textbook accent.
- **Give the distinctive sounds room.** Sindhi has implosive consonants that most other Indian languages lack, like the sounds written ڳ and ڊ in the Perso-Arabic script. Pronounce them clearly, the way you would in careful conversation, and they come through in the text.
- **Avoid mixing languages mid-sentence.** If you switch between Sindhi and another language in a single sentence, the transcript is more likely to blur; keeping a sentence in one language gives the clearest result.
- **Keep background noise low.** A quiet room matters more than a fancy microphone; the model works best when your voice is the clearest thing it hears.
- **Edit before you copy.** The transcript box is fully editable, so fix any slip before you paste the text into a document or message.
