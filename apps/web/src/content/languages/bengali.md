Bengali is spoken by about 100 million people in India — across West Bengal, Tripura, the Barak Valley of Assam, and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands — and it is one of the great literary languages of the subcontinent. It also has one of the most distinctive scripts in India: an abugida with rounded letterforms, its own set of vowel signs, and complex conjunct characters that can make typing slow on a phone keyboard. That is exactly why **Bengali speech to text** earns its place: speaking is faster than typing, and Veena makes speaking-to-text work entirely on your device. The [speech to text hub](/speech-to-text/) shows where Bengali fits among all 22 scheduled languages of India.

## Bengali speech to text with Veena

Veena's recognition engine is built on IndicConformer-600M-Multilingual from AI4Bharat, exported to ONNX and quantized so it runs entirely on-device. Veena's multilingual engine covers all 22 scheduled languages of India, and Bengali is transcribed with a dedicated vocabulary and decoding tuned for it. Tap the microphone, speak in Bengali, and the transcript appears in an editable text box — no internet connection needed, and nothing uploaded anywhere.

The script matters here. Bengali has its own vowel marks, conjuncts, and numerals, and Veena outputs your speech in the Bengali script rather than a transliteration, so what you see is what a Bengali reader expects. If a word comes out spelled differently than you intended, the transcript is editable — tap the word and retype it without re-dictating the sentence.

Veena records 16 kHz mono audio and transcribes it locally with the on-device model, so you can even use it in places with poor connectivity.

## How to use Veena for Bengali

On mobile, install Veena for Android or iOS and grant microphone permission — the only permission the app requests. Tap the microphone and speak; hold-to-record suits short messages, while tap-to-record lets you dictate a full paragraph without holding the screen. The transcript lands in an editable text box, ready to copy, share, or fix. A replay button plays back the last recording, which is handy for checking a tricky name or number.

On desktop, The Veena desktop app for Windows takes a different approach: a push-to-talk global shortcut captures your speech from any application, and the Bengali text is pasted directly into the app you are using — or copied to your clipboard. A local recording history keeps past dictations organized, the model for your language downloads inside the app, and custom models plus optional post-processing (filler-word removal, custom-word correction) are available when your vocabulary is specialized.

## Tips for better accuracy in Bengali

Bengali spans a wide variety of accents, from the speech of Kolkata to the dialects of Tripura and the Barak Valley. A few habits keep transcripts clean:

- Speak in your natural accent. Do not try to sound like a newsreader; Veena is tuned for everyday Bengali.
- Use complete sentences. Context helps the engine resolve homophones and choose the right word.
- Keep a consistent pace and pause between sentences so the transcript stays well separated.
- Reduce background noise and keep the microphone close — a headset on desktop helps a lot.
- Avoid switching languages mid-sentence. Bengali speakers often mix in English or Hindi words; accuracy is highest when each utterance stays in one language.
- Check names and technical terms with the replay feature, and edit the transcript directly when a word needs correcting.
