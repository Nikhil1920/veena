Dogri is spoken by about 2.5 million people in the Jammu region of Jammu and Kashmir, with speakers in Himachal Pradesh and Punjab as well. Written in the Devanagari script, it is one of the 22 scheduled languages of India, with its own literature, music, and media. Yet like most languages outside the biggest few, Dogri is rarely the first language added to commercial speech products. Veena takes a different approach: **Dogri speech to text** that runs entirely on your own device, free of charge and free of the cloud. The [speech to text hub](/speech-to-text/) lists Dogri among the 22 scheduled languages the Veena engine covers.

## Dogri speech to text with Veena

Veena's recognition engine is built on IndicConformer-600M-Multilingual from AI4Bharat, exported to ONNX and quantized so it can run entirely on-device. Veena's multilingual engine covers all 22 scheduled languages of India, and Dogri is transcribed with a dedicated vocabulary and decoding tuned for it. For a language community of 2.5 million, on-device processing is not a luxury; it is the difference between a tool that works everywhere and one that only works near a reliable connection. Veena records 16 kHz mono audio and transcribes it locally with the on-device model, so your transcripts are produced on your phone or computer, and your audio never leaves your device.

## Why transcribe Dogri audio

Dogri is a language with a strong identity — its own folk songs, theater, and a growing body of written work — and voice transcription helps it in concrete ways:

- Teachers and students in the Jammu region can take notes and prepare material in Dogri without slow Devanagari typing.
- Journalists and writers covering local news can file stories in Dogri, hands-free.
- Local administration and offices that communicate in Dogri can produce letters and records by dictation.
- Families can transcribe interviews, folk songs, and oral histories in Dogri before the recordings fade.
- Content creators making Dogri videos and audio programs can generate scripts and captions by voice.
- Anyone who types slowly in Devanagari can simply speak instead.

The underlying point is the same one that motivates Veena's whole language list: a smaller community deserves native speech to text just as much as a larger one. When speakers can dictate, they write more in Dogri rather than switching to Hindi or English — and the written language grows.

## Tips for better accuracy in Dogri

Dogri varies across the Jammu region, Himachal Pradesh, and Punjab, so the best results come from consistent, natural speech:

- Speak in your own accent. Do not imitate a formal or broadcast style; natural Dogri is what the engine is tuned for.
- Use complete sentences rather than isolated words, so the engine can use context.
- Keep a steady pace and pause between sentences.
- Reduce background noise and keep the microphone close — a headset helps on desktop.
- Avoid switching languages mid-sentence. Dogri speakers often mix in Hindi, Punjabi, or English; accuracy is highest when each utterance stays in one language.
- Replay tricky passages and edit the transcript directly when a spelling needs correction.
